  Native 3D validation covers two layers and a limited set
of vertical fabrics. More layers, different vertical resource
densities, and heterogeneous sampling patterns require ad-
ditional evaluation. Shared hosts and three runtime repeats
limit inference about small differences; repeats do not replace
independent circuits or layouts. The clma stage is short, and a
preserved storage-preﬂight pause separates one BNN-2D stage
pair. No rerun was substituted, but the interruption further
limits interpretation of small stage differences. The substantive
results are the large construction reductions, scaling beyond
the group width, preserved native semantics, and measured
beneﬁts on qualiﬁed complete ﬂows.

                   VIII. CONCLUSION

  Multicore routing support does not by  itself expose the
parallelism available during lookahead construction. We de-
compose layer-aware  native  construction  into independent
ﬂoods, original-order cost reduction, and explicitly bounded
execution/commit interfaces. Cross-group scheduling removes
the four-sample group bottleneck and enables approximately
twelvefold one-to-sixteen-core construction scaling on two
large 3D graphs. At eight cores, GEMM process speedup is
1.638 ± 0.005×; a qualiﬁed large 3D subset and independent
2D architectures corroborate full-ﬂow beneﬁt. Direct global-
loop controls identify the source of the gain, while slice and
storage studies expose further resource tradeoffs. The resulting
execution contract provides an equivalent, measurable founda-
tion for faster 3D architecture exploration and future hardware-
parallel routing-cost computation.
