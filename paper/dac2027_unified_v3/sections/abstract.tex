Changing a 3D FPGA's routing resources or indexed costs can require rebuilding the router's layer-aware lookahead. Each sampled search contributes a source-relative pair of delay and congestion costs. We present two parallelization regimes with different semantic constraints. STRICT schedules independent native searches across groups and consumes results in their original order. Under explicit conditions on recording, ties and interpolation, it preserves the native wire map. ALT reorganizes each search into waves of whole-pair FP32 updates. CPU and CUDA backends implement the same rule and receive independent route qualification. On three two-layer routing graphs, each workload completes six configurations with three matched repeats on eight physical CPU cores and an H100. ALT CUDA achieves a \constructionspeedup{} over Stock of $20.03\times$ on GEMM, $19.97\times$ on BNN and $28.06\times$ on LSTM. The corresponding values of \processspeedup{} are $1.80\times$, $1.88\times$ and $2.28\times$. All 54 formal routes passed independent legality and timing checks with zero overuse and met the Stock-relative delay and wirelength limits.
