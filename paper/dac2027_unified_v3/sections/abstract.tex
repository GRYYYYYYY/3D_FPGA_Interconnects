Changing a 3D FPGA's interconnect or indexed costs can require rebuilding its layer-aware routing lookahead. Each sampled wire search records a delay and its associated congestion cost relative to its own source. We separate sample identity from scheduling to expose two kinds of parallelism. STRICT overlaps private native searches across groups, bounds the number of live results and consumes them in the original order. It preserves the native wire map under explicit conditions on visitation, arithmetic, ties and finalization. ALT instead reorganizes each search into snapshot waves that compare whole FP32 cost pairs and propagate improvements until the current bucket closes. Its CPU and GPU backends implement the same rule. On three two-layer routing graphs with eight physical CPU cores and an H100, STRICT Pipeline achieves $6.28$--$6.58\times$ \constructionspeedup{} over Stock. ALT GPU reaches $20.03\times$ on GEMM, $19.97\times$ on BNN and $28.06\times$ on LSTM. The corresponding \processspeedup{} values are $1.80\times$, $1.88\times$ and $2.28\times$ from native launch to exit. All 54 formal routes passed independent legality and timing checks with zero overuse and met the Stock-relative critical-path and wirelength limits.
