\section{Introduction}
Stacked logic, memory blocks and programmable inter-layer connections give 3D FPGA architects choices in both wire geometry and the cost of crossing layers. Architecture generation, layer-aware placement and multi-die routing expose these choices to CAD tools~\cite{lazagna,flatland,multidie}. A change to connectivity or indexed costs can invalidate a cached routing lookahead. For the new configuration, VPR samples representative wires and builds a table that the router queries repeatedly for the remaining delay and congestion cost to a pin on a given layer (Fig.~\ref{fig:motivation}).

However, a parallel router can still spend much of its runtime constructing that table. The evaluated native map builder searches one wire sample at a time, even when subsequent net routing uses parallel workers. Construction takes 46.6--59.2\% of Stock \processtime{} on our three two-layer workloads. Two dependencies constrain acceleration. Searches from different sources can reach the same pin yet populate different displacement cells. Within one cell, equal-delay paths can carry different congestion costs, and native insertion retains the first pair. Pooling sources or reducing in completion order can therefore change the router's input.

Architecture-adaptive estimators cluster wires or sample representative starts to build cost-to-target maps~\cite{adaptive_astar,diversity,air}. Multi-die models account for boundary crossings~\cite{multidie}. MultiQueue parallelizes connection searches and proposes faster Dijkstra searches to sample more starts within a time budget~\cite{multiqueue}. We ask how much parallelism is available when the graph, costs and sampled starts are fixed and the output must still support the native layer-aware lookup.

We separate each sample's identity from its execution order. STRICT runs private native searches across groups and consumes results in original order. Under explicit tie, arithmetic and finalization conditions, this preserves the native wire map. ALT changes the search rule: snapshot waves compare whole FP32 pairs and reopen improved nodes until their bucket closes. We qualify its CPU and GPU implementations independently of STRICT. On GEMM, BNN and LSTM, STRICT Pipeline achieves a \constructionspeedup{} of $6.28$--$6.58\times$ over Stock. ALT GPU reaches $20.03\times$, $19.97\times$ and $28.06\times$, with corresponding \processspeedup{} of $1.80\times$, $1.88\times$ and $2.28\times$.
