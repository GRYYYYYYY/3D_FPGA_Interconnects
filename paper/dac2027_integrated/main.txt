     VI. LIMITATIONS AND HARDWARE BOUNDARY

  The STRICT proposition covers wire-map semantics under
explicit assumptions. ALT provides measured CPU/CUDA
agreement and qualiﬁed downstream behavior on the tested
inputs, with congestion counterexamples to native equivalence.
Wider seed, architecture and numerical-domain claims would
require new evidence. LSTM is an ALT-tuning holdout from
the same architecture family and previously appeared in the
CPU study. Shared instances, small repetition counts and un-
controlled ﬁlesystem caches limit interpretation of variability
and deployment performance.
  No FPGA hardware superiority  is veriﬁed. The current
candidate lacks an achieved implementation frequency and
calibrated dependent random whole-pair RMW service. U280
logical storage totals and software cache hit counts do not
establish ports, arbitration, ﬁnite queues, cross-SLR place-
ment or service throughput. The observed environment has
no  visible FPGA or usable  installed platform. The hard-
ware state remains UNRESOLVED: speciﬁcally, no supported
memory-service evidence. Both board-measured acceleration
and FPGA-superiority ﬂags remain false.
 A separate ofﬂine compact-CUDA E1 target and modeled
proposal trafﬁc imply necessary service bounds, not native
hardware performance; compact/direct native qualiﬁcation is
not inherited. Restarting hardware work would require an
actual target/shell or calibrated facility, dependent random 32-
byte RMW with ﬁnite outstanding requests, a complete physi-
cal/timing budget, and end-to-end margin against the strongest
matched software baseline. This paper’s measured contribution
is CPU/GPU lookahead construction, not successful FPGA
acceleration or a proof that every FPGA mapping must fail.
